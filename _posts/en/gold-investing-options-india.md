Gold can be worn as jewellery or held as part of an investment portfolio, but the same purchase method may not suit both purposes. Its price can rise or fall, and gold itself does not pay interest or dividends. Although many people consider gold a hedge against inflation or a resource in emergencies, neither protection nor immediate liquidity is guaranteed.

Before buying, decide whether your goal is **wearing jewellery or investing**. Common options in India include physical gold such as jewellery, coins and bars; digital gold; gold mutual funds, often structured as funds of funds; and gold exchange-traded funds (ETFs). Their structures, costs, liquidity and investor protections differ.

## First, clarify your purpose

- **Consumption:** Buying jewellery to wear for a wedding, celebration or everyday use.
- **Investment:** Seeking exposure to gold-price movements or diversification across asset classes.

Jewellery, coins and bars can involve product premiums, buy–sell spreads and storage costs. Gold ETFs and funds offer gold-linked investment exposure but do not provide jewellery to wear.

## Four ways to buy or invest in gold

### 1. Physical gold: jewellery, coins and bars

Jewellery can be worn and held directly. Coins and bars are other forms of physical gold. A jewellery bill may include design and making charges, wastage and other seller charges in addition to the gold price. Coins and bars may also carry production premiums or buy–sell spreads.

Cash resale and exchange for new jewellery depend on the seller's rules. Making charges and other purchase costs may not be recovered; even if gold prices rise, your resale proceeds need not rise by the same amount. Compare the buyback and exchange terms.

**Potential advantages**

- Jewellery can be used or worn, and physical gold is a tangible asset held directly.
- Eligible jewellery may be pledged for a loan, subject to the lender's valuation and terms.

**Costs and risks**

- Making and wastage charges vary; the original article cites an illustrative range of 10%–30% for jewellery, not a universal rate. Taxes and other charges may also apply.
- Home storage carries theft or loss risk, and bank lockers have charges and their own terms.
- Resale value depends on gold weight and purity; stones may not be credited at their full purchase price. Exchange deductions can apply even to hallmarked jewellery.

Check the applicable BIS hallmark, fineness or purity marking and HUID, and obtain an itemised invoice. The [BIS hallmarking FAQ](https://www.bis.gov.in/hallmarking-overview/hallmarking-faqs/hallmarking-faq/?lang=en) describes the six-character HUID and the BIS Care app's verification feature. Hallmarking provides purity and identification information; it does not guarantee returns or resale price. Check coin and bar purity, invoice, premium and buyback terms too.

### 2. Digital gold

Some apps and providers offer small online purchases of digital gold. The account typically displays a quantity associated with the amount paid. Purity, custody, insurance, sale and physical-delivery arrangements vary by provider and contract. Some providers quote 24-karat, 99.9% purity and offer purchases from as little as ₹1, but check the specific offer.

**Potential advantages**

- Small purchases may be convenient without storing gold at home.
- Depending on the provider's terms, you may be able to sell or request delivery of a coin after meeting minimum requirements.

**Costs and risks**

- Digital gold is not a SEBI-regulated mutual fund or gold ETF; the protections applicable to those products do not automatically apply. Check the current regulatory position and the provider's contractual protections.
- GST, buy–sell spreads, storage or delivery charges, minimum redemption quantities and time limits may apply. Check the current tax treatment and price breakdown.
- Identify the seller and custodian, how ownership is recorded, what insurance covers and how redemption works if the service or contract ends.
- Convenience alone is not evidence of safety or regulatory oversight. Check current terms, charges and complaint procedures before choosing a provider.

### 3. Gold mutual fund or gold fund of funds

In India, a gold mutual fund is often a fund of funds (FoF) that invests in gold ETF units. Confirm the actual holdings in the scheme documents. Depending on the scheme, you can invest through an asset management company (AMC) or mutual fund platform, including by SIP, generally without a demat account.

**Potential advantages**

- A mutual fund folio can provide gold-linked exposure without managing exchange trades or physical storage.
- Some schemes allow regular SIPs with small contributions.

**Costs and risks**

- An FoF may add costs to those of the underlying ETF. Review the total expense ratio (TER), exit load and tracking difference in the scheme documents.
- Purchases and redemptions use the applicable NAV under the scheme's cutoff and settlement rules, rather than intraday exchange trading.
- Gold prices can fall; an SIP does not guarantee profit. The scheme still has custody, operational and market risks.

Read the [guide to mutual funds and market-linked products](../equity-investments-in-india/) and explore the [SIP calculator](../../calculators/mf_sip_calculator.html). Calculator estimates do not guarantee future returns.

### 4. Gold ETF

Gold ETFs are funds whose units trade on an exchange. The original article cites a SEBI requirement to hold at least 95% of net assets in gold and permitted gold-related instruments; check the current rules and the scheme's documents for its holdings, risks and expenses. A unit's denomination and price vary by scheme.

**Potential advantages**

- Units can provide gold-linked exposure without jewellery making or wastage charges or home storage.
- ETFs operate within the applicable SEBI mutual fund framework.

**Costs and risks**

- Exchange trading generally requires demat and trading accounts. Brokerage, bid–ask spread, demat fees and fund expenses can apply.
- An illustrative expense-ratio range in the original article is 0.3%–0.8%; actual costs vary. Market prices can differ from NAV and liquidity varies.
- Gold prices can fall, and returns are not guaranteed. Electronic holdings avoid physical home storage but do not eliminate custody, operational or market risks.
- Exchange sales generally provide cash, not jewellery. Any physical redemption facility depends on the scheme's terms and eligibility.

## Comparison

| Feature | Jewellery, coins and bars | Digital gold | Gold ETF | Gold mutual fund / FoF |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Form** | Physical gold; jewellery can be worn | Provider-recorded gold holding | Exchange-traded fund units | Mutual fund units, often invested in gold ETFs |
| **Costs** | Premium, making or wastage charges where applicable, tax and storage | Tax, spread and possible storage or delivery charges | Fund expenses, brokerage, spread and demat fees | Fund expenses, possible underlying ETF costs and exit load |
| **Minimum** | Depends on item and seller | Depends on provider; some offer small purchases | Unit price and trading requirements vary | Scheme and SIP minimums vary |
| **Storage or custody** | Owner must arrange safe storage | Provider's custody and contract apply | Electronic units; fund custody arrangements apply | Folio units; scheme custody arrangements apply |
| **Demat account** | Not required | Generally not required | Generally required for exchange trading | Generally not required |
| **Oversight** | Check applicable hallmarking and consumer rules | Check current rules and provider terms; not an ETF or mutual fund | SEBI mutual fund framework | SEBI mutual fund framework |

Purity, prices, charges and eligibility vary. Compare current scheme documents and quotes rather than relying on a fixed unit price or expense figure.

## Which option might suit you?

### For use or wearing

If you need jewellery for a wedding, festival or everyday use, physical jewellery serves that purpose. Check the applicable hallmark and purity, itemised costs and buyback terms. Coins are another physical form but are not wearable jewellery.

If the purchase is several years away, you might consider gold-linked funds until you need to buy jewellery. Their value may fall, however, and eventual jewellery making charges, taxes and other costs still apply. Compare those risks and costs with buying now.

### For investment

A gold ETF may suit someone comfortable using a demat and trading account. A gold mutual fund may suit someone who prefers a folio or regular SIP without managing exchange trades. Neither is automatically the cheapest or safest option; compare total costs, liquidity, tracking and suitability. Jewellery may be less efficient for investment because of purchase charges and resale deductions.

Gold can be one part of a diversified portfolio, but there is no single allocation percentage appropriate for everyone. Consider your goals, time horizon, capacity for loss and existing investments, and read the official product documents before investing.
