A **chit fund** is a traditional group savings and borrowing arrangement that has been used in India for generations. It can act both as a savings tool and as a way to borrow for urgent financial needs.

Members contribute instalments for a fixed period, and one member receives the pooled amount each period. In a typical auction, members who need money sooner bid by offering a discount. A chit can therefore be a way to save or to obtain funds early, subject to the group's rules. Neither returns nor access to credit are guaranteed: the written terms of the particular scheme matter.

<h2 id="how-chit-funds-work">1. How does an auction chit fund work?</h2>

A chit fund has a fixed number of members and a specified term. It usually has subscribers, a **foreman** or operator, a chit value and a duration set out in an agreement. For example, 20 members contributing ₹5,000 each for 20 months form a chit with a value of ₹1,00,000. This is commonly called a ₹5,000 chit.

1. **Members and contributions:** In this example, 20 people agree to contribute ₹5,000 each month for 20 months, giving a total chit value of ₹1,00,000.
2. **Reverse auction:** Members bid on a specified date each month. A member who needs money sooner offers a discount, and the member offering the highest discount may receive that month's prize. Under the amended central Chit Funds Act, the maximum auction discount may be up to **40%**. Check applicable state amendments, rules and the particular agreement.
3. **Foreman's commission:** The law permits a commission of up to **7%** of the chit value—₹7,000 on ₹1,00,000.
4. **Dividend distribution:** After the foreman's 7% commission is deducted from the auction discount in this example, the remainder is divided equally among the 20 members. This reduces the net instalment.

The prize paid to the winning bidder is determined after subtracting the discount and deductions permitted by the agreement and applicable law from the chit value. The foreman's commission and permitted expenses may be deducted from the discount; the balance is distributed as a dividend. Check the calculation of discounts, commission, expenses and dividends in the agreement. Do not assume one discount or commission rate applies to every scheme.

**Instalments do not stop after receiving the prize.** The winning member must continue to pay the remaining instalments. Whether a guarantor or collateral is required before payment depends on the agreement and applicable rules. Do not assume that a government employee's guarantee, property deed or fixed-deposit document is mandatory in every scheme. A guarantor and collateral are different forms of security.

<h2 id="chit-fund-benefits">2. Potential benefits</h2>

* **Dual purpose:** One arrangement can offer a way to save and a way to borrow.
* **An alternative source of finance:** Some schemes may require income documents as well as a guarantor or collateral. Eligibility and payout conditions depend on the operator and agreement.
* **Savings discipline:** Regular monthly contributions can encourage small-business owners and homemakers to set money aside.
* **Possible early access:** A member who wins the auction and satisfies documentation or security conditions may receive the prize before the term ends.
* **Ability to calculate costs:** If the discount, commission, dividends and instalment terms are clear, the total cost can be calculated.

These are not guaranteed benefits. The timing of an auction win, monthly discounts, missed payments and the operator's conduct all affect the outcome. A chit fund is not a safe, fixed-return product like a bank FD or RD.

<h2 id="chit-fund-credit-risks">3. Credit risks</h2>

### A. Foreman or operator risk

* **Fraud and mismanagement:** An unregistered or informal operator may misuse the funds and disappear. Poor recordkeeping, delayed payouts or closure can also cause losses.
* **Limited legal protection:** Recovering money from a chit conducted without the required Registrar of Chits approval can be difficult. Verify the registration and approval of the **specific chit** with the relevant state's registrar. Company registration or the word “registered” in an advertisement alone is not proof.

### B. Participant or subscriber risk

* **Default:** A member who takes the prize early may stop paying later instalments, causing cash-flow problems for the remaining members. A winning bidder may therefore be asked for a guarantor or collateral. Before signing, clarify when a guarantor's responsibility begins and which amounts it covers.
* Security requirements vary by scheme. Do not assume all registered companies require a government employee as guarantor, a property deed or a cheque.

<h2 id="other-chit-risks">4. Other risks</h2>

### A. Timing and the need for cash

Someone who needs funds immediately may have to offer a large discount, reducing the amount received. There is no guarantee of when a bid will succeed. Likewise, the total dividends received by someone who waits until the final month are not guaranteed in advance.

### B. Early withdrawal and other restrictions

The agreement should explain leaving midway, missed instalments, withdrawal after receiving the prize, transfer of membership and final-month payment. Do not put an emergency fund into a chit assuming you can withdraw it immediately.

<h2 id="chit-fund-example">5. A 20-month example</h2>

**Members:** 20 · **Basic monthly instalment:** ₹5,000 · **Chit value:** ₹1,00,000 · **Foreman's commission at 7%:** ₹7,000.

In this illustration, each monthly discount is at least ₹7,000. The distributable dividend is the auction discount minus the ₹7,000 commission. Other charges are excluded.

* **Total chit value:** ₹1,00,000 (20 months × ₹5,000).
* **Total net instalments paid:** ₹93,875 after ₹6,125 in dividends.
* **Saver receiving the prize in month 20:** ₹93,000, with an illustrative annual XIRR of approximately **−1.21%**.
* **Borrower receiving the prize in month 2:** illustrative annual borrowing-cost XIRR of approximately **38.09%**.

<h2 id="chit-fund-xirr">6. XIRR: returns and borrowing costs</h2>

Because monthly chit-fund cash flows are uneven, **XIRR (Extended Internal Rate of Return)** can be used to calculate an annualised return or borrowing cost using actual dates.

This example assumes each cash flow occurs on the first day of the month, from 1 November 2026 in month 1 to 1 June 2028 in month 20. The net instalment is the ₹5,000 basic instalment less that month's dividend per member.

### A. Saver receiving the prize in month 20

* **Cash flow:** The saver pays ₹4,100 to ₹5,000 each month, a total outflow of ₹93,875, and receives ₹93,000 in month 20.
* **Annual XIRR:** Approximately **−1.21%**.
* **Analysis:** In this hypothetical example, net instalments exceed the final payout by ₹875, resulting in a negative XIRR. Actual returns depend on auction discounts, commissions and dividends.

### B. Borrower receiving the prize in month 2

* **Cash flow:** For the borrowing-cost XIRR, payments are positive and the prize received is negative. The member pays ₹5,000 in month 1. In month 2, the ₹75,000 prize less the ₹4,100 net instalment leaves **₹70,900**. The member then pays the net instalments shown for months 3 to 20.
* **Annual borrowing-cost XIRR:** Approximately **38.09%**.
* **Analysis:** The first instalment paid before the auction is included. Because the cash-flow signs change more than once—an initial payment, then a prize, followed by further payments—there can be more than one mathematical root. The 38.09% root is the one found using a 10% starting guess. Actual auction and payment dates, dividend timing and other charges change the cost. Compare cash flows on their actual dates with alternatives, including personal loans.

<h2 id="who-chit-funds-suit">7. Who might a chit fund suit?</h2>

People who struggle to obtain conventional credit because of a low credit score may consider a chit as an alternative. Eligibility, security requirements, success at auction and payment timing depend on the operator, agreement and applicable rules. Credit is not guaranteed.

1. **People with a low or no credit score:** Lenders' minimum scores and other eligibility requirements vary; there is no universal 750 threshold. Chit operators may also assess credit history, repayment capacity, guarantors or collateral. A low CIBIL score alone does not guarantee a prize or credit.
2. **Informal workers and small businesses:** People without formal income-tax returns or salary slips may use chits for business expansion or working capital.
3. **Planned family expenses:** A chit may be considered for known expenses such as a wedding or education fees in the next one or two years.

It may be suitable only if you have reliable cash flow to pay throughout the term, can tolerate uncertainty about the timing and amount of the payout, and have verified the particular chit and compared its cost with alternatives.

> **Important:** A chit fund may be a practical borrowing option, but it is not necessarily the best investment for building wealth.

<h2 id="chit-fund-regulations">8. Regulations and official links</h2>

Chit funds in India are governed by the **Chit Funds Act, 1982** and the relevant state government's rules. Check which legal provisions, registration procedures and auction limits apply to the particular scheme under current law.

* **Reserve Bank of India (RBI):** Although chit-fund companies are classified as NBFCs, their day-to-day operations are supervised by the respective state's Registrar of Chits, not the RBI. See the [RBI FAQs on NBFCs and financial entities](https://www.rbi.org.in/nbfcfaqs).
* **Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI):** SEBI issues guidance concerning unregistered collective investment schemes and illegal Ponzi schemes. See the [SEBI official portal](https://www.sebi.gov.in).
* **Safety:** Use only a chit fund registered and licensed with the state government. Company or GST registration, or another type of RBI registration, does not by itself prove approval for a particular chit.
* Do not assume RBI or SEBI complaint portals apply to every chit-fund dispute. If you suspect an unregulated deposit-taking or fundraising scheme, consult [RBI Sachet](https://sachet.rbi.org.in/) for complaints or guidance; the appropriate authority depends on the scheme.

<h2 id="chit-fund-checklist">9. Checklist before joining</h2>

1. **Chit details:** Obtain proof of registration or approval for the particular chit or group, its registration number and an official way to verify it.
2. **Complete agreement:** Check the chit value, members, term, payment dates, auction procedure, maximum and minimum bid rules, commission, expenses and dividend distribution.
3. **After winning:** When is the prize paid? What documents, guarantor or collateral are needed? How are remaining instalments paid?
4. **Missed payments:** What late fees, loss of rights, removal or guarantor liabilities apply?
5. **Leaving midway:** When and how much is repaid? What deductions, replacement-member rules and deadlines apply?
6. **Records:** Will you receive monthly statements of auction results, contributions, dividends, costs and balances? Obtain receipts for every instalment.
7. **Affordability:** Can you keep paying if income falls or an emergency arises? Do not use the chit as a substitute for an emergency fund.
8. **Cost comparison:** If borrowing, compare the actual amount received and cash-flow dates with the total cost of alternatives, including a personal loan. Do not rely solely on verbal promises of “low interest” or “guaranteed profit”.
