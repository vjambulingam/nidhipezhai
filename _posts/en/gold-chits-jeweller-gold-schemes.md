Monthly gold savings schemes offered by jewellers are often called **“gold chits”** or **jewellery savings schemes**. A customer generally pays instalments for a specified period and buys jewellery at maturity, subject to the scheme's terms. The duration, instalments, bonus, purchase process and right to a cash refund vary by provider and scheme.

The word “chit” alone does not establish that a scheme is a registered chit under the *Chit Funds Act, 1982*. Nor can every such scheme be classified as the same type of advance-payment contract. Its legal classification may depend on its actual structure, the entity running it and the contract. Rely on written terms rather than advertising labels, and seek qualified legal or financial advice if needed.

## 1. How does a gold savings scheme work?

Terms are not uniform, but common features include:

1. **Duration and instalments:** Check the number of instalments, each due date and the consequences of late or missed payments.
2. **Bonus or discount:** Some schemes offer a final-instalment benefit, extra purchase credit or a discount on making charges. Eligibility, limits and exclusions depend on the written terms.
3. **Maturity and redemption:** The scheme determines whether you receive jewellery, gold coins, store credit or cash. Find out in advance whether cash refunds are allowed, what happens if you leave early and how missing a deadline affects your rights.

---

## 2. Common types of schemes

The methods below appear in some schemes. Do not assume the terms from a scheme's name or advertising.

### a. Value-based scheme

* **How it works:** Each instalment is recorded as a monetary credit.
* **Maturity:** Eligible credit, together with any applicable bonus or discount, can be used to buy jewellery.
* **Gold price:** Check which day's price applies and how it is calculated. If the price rises, the same credit may buy less gold by weight.

### b. Weight-based or gold accumulation scheme

* **How it works:** The scheme may say that each instalment earns a weight or gold credit. Check the price, purity and charges used in the calculation.
* **Maturity:** The final credited weight, how it can be used and any costs of buying jewellery depend on the contract.
* **Note:** Paying instalments on different dates can average the purchase price, but does not guarantee protection from rising prices or a profit. A weight credit does not necessarily remove making charges, wastage or other fees.

## 3. A simple example: credit is not a return

Consider this hypothetical example:

* **Monthly instalment:** ₹5,000.
* **Number of instalments:** 11. This is only an example, not a standard or legal limit.
* **Customer contributions:** 11 × ₹5,000 = ₹55,000.
* **Hypothetical benefit:** ₹5,000 in purchase credit if the customer meets the scheme's conditions.
* **Total jewellery purchase credit:** ₹60,000.

| Description | Amount |
| :--- | ---: |
| Customer contributions | ₹55,000 |
| Conditional benefit | ₹5,000 |
| Credit available for jewellery | ₹60,000 |

This illustrates how a benefit might be presented; it is not the terms of an actual scheme or an investment return. Payments are made in instalments and the final benefit may be restricted purchase credit or jewellery rather than cash. An annual return cannot be inferred from these figures alone without valuing the actual benefits, costs and timing. Jewellery price, making charges, wastage, stones, taxes and other fees affect the final cost. Compare the written terms and an itemised bill on the purchase date.

## 4. Potential benefits

* **Planned saving:** Instalments may help set money aside for an already-planned jewellery purchase.
* **Possible discount:** Some schemes offer discounts on certain items or charges; limits and exclusions matter.
* **Convenient payments:** Payment methods and account information vary by provider. Keep an official receipt or record for every instalment.

## 5. Who might find a scheme suitable?

| May be worth considering | May not be suitable |
| :--- | :--- |
| The contract is clear and you already plan to buy jewellery from that store within the specified period. | You need savings that can easily be converted into cash in an emergency. |
| You can afford the instalments and understand the benefit's conditions and additional costs. | You want an investment linked to gold prices without restrictions on jewellery purchases. |
| You understand the risk of store closure or service disruption and can bear the loss of the amount paid. | You need a secure deposit or a guaranteed return. |

If your goal is jewellery, compare the scheme's total cost with buying the same item directly. If you seek gold-price exposure, consider [gold investment options](../gold-investing-options-india/) separately rather than assuming a jeweller's scheme is an investment substitute. ETFs and mutual funds also have market and product-specific risks.

## 6. Jeweller credit risk

Think of the money paid into the scheme as a claim against the jeweller or the entity operating it. If that entity stops operating, obtaining your money, credit or jewellery may be delayed or result in a loss. Your rights depend on the legal classification, contract and status of the entity that received the money.

Do not treat the scheme as equivalent to a bank deposit or assume it has the same protection or insurance. If an advertisement claims specific regulation or protection, verify it with the relevant official source. A long history, well-known name or stock exchange listing alone does not guarantee that scheme contributions will be recovered.

## 7. Legal classification and complaints

The applicable law and complaint channel cannot be determined from the scheme's name alone. They can depend on its structure, who receives the money and the contract. Do not assume a particular time limit or legal rule applies to every scheme; check current official rules and seek qualified legal advice where needed.

* **RBI Sachet:** Check whether the [RBI Sachet portal](https://sachet.rbi.org.in/) is relevant if you suspect an unregulated deposit or financial scheme.
* **SEBI:** The [SEBI website](https://www.sebi.gov.in/) and [SCORES complaint portal](https://scores.sebi.gov.in/) are relevant only where the entity or product falls within SEBI's jurisdiction.
* First complain to the provider in writing and retain receipts, the contract and communications. The appropriate consumer or regulatory authority for the next step depends on the entity and the issue.

## 8. Checklist before joining

1. **Who is the contracting party?** Check the full name, address, contact details and grievance officer on the receipts and terms. A popular name or long history alone is not proof of safety.
2. **What happens in each situation?** Ask in writing about credit, refunds, deadlines and deductions for late or missed instalments, early exit, failure to purchase at maturity, or store or scheme closure.
3. **What can you receive at maturity?** Cash, jewellery or credit? By when must it be used? Can it be transferred to another branch, person or item? Is there a minimum or compulsory purchase?
4. **What is the actual value of the benefit?** Check the bonus, permitted uses, excluded products or designs, and limits on making charges or wastage.
5. **How are price and weight calculated?** Ask in writing when the gold price is fixed and how purity, weight, credit and price changes are calculated.
6. **What is the full bill?** Request a sample bill including making charges, wastage, stones or other materials, taxes and other fees. Verify applicable tax rates and calculations against current official rules and the final bill; do not assume one percentage applies in every case.
7. **How is purity certified?** Check applicable BIS hallmark and purity details when buying jewellery. A hallmark concerns gold purity; it does not guarantee repayment of scheme contributions or investment safety.
8. **What evidence should you keep?** Save signed terms, every instalment receipt, account statements, advertised promises and written communications with the provider.
