Insurance is a contract that can help cover financial loss when a specified risk occurs, subject to the policy's terms. No policy covers every expense. Coverage, sum insured, exclusions, waiting periods and co-payments vary by insurer and plan. Here are seven common types of insurance in India.

## 1. Life insurance

Life insurance provides a benefit according to the contract if the insured person dies during the policy term. The benefit is paid to the designated nominee or beneficiary, subject to the applicable policy and legal provisions. Some plans may also provide a maturity or investment-linked benefit, depending on their terms.

- **Term insurance:** Provides life cover for a specified period. Many pure protection term plans do not pay a maturity benefit if the insured survives the term; return-of-premium variants have separate conditions.
- **Savings- or investment-linked plans:** May provide a maturity benefit or investment value. Understand the protection, charges and any non-guaranteed returns separately.

Estimate the cover you need by considering dependants, outstanding debt, future responsibilities and existing insurance. Fully disclose your health, smoking habits, occupation and previous insurance details in the application, and keep nominee details up to date.

Use the [life insurance needs calculator](../../calculators/life-insurance-sum-assured-calculator.html) to estimate cover based on household expenses, debt, future goals and existing assets.

## 2. Health or medical insurance

Health insurance can help cover eligible hospital expenses within the sum insured and policy conditions. An individual plan covers one person; a family floater allows covered family members to share one sum insured.

**Check:** Waiting periods for pre-existing conditions and specific treatments, co-payments, deductibles, room-rent limits, disease or treatment sub-limits, network hospitals and pre-authorisation for cashless treatment. Cashless access does not mean every treatment is automatically approved in full; final approval depends on policy conditions.

Disclose existing conditions and treatment history accurately. Health insurance that reimburses medical expenses is not the same as a fixed-benefit critical illness policy.

## 3. Accident insurance

An accident policy may pay specified benefits for death or disability resulting from a sudden, unforeseen accident. Depending on the plan, additional benefits may cover permanent total disability, permanent partial disability, temporary total disability or accident-related medical expenses.

It is generally not a substitute for cover for death from natural causes or for all illnesses. Check how the policy defines an accident, how disability percentages are assessed, which documents are required and what waiting periods or exclusions apply. Some benefits may need to be selected separately.

## 4. Critical illness insurance

This cover may pay a fixed amount if a listed critical illness is diagnosed and meets the policy's precise definitions and eligibility conditions. It is generally not a reimbursement policy based on hospital bills.

Read the list of illnesses, definitions of early or advanced stages, waiting periods, any survival-period requirement, pre-existing disease rules and reports needed for a claim. A plan called “critical illness” does not automatically cover every serious illness.

## 5. Disability insurance

Disability insurance or an additional benefit can help with income loss if illness or an accident affects your ability to work. Depending on the product, a specified disability may trigger a lump sum, income benefit or a rider that waives loan or insurance premiums. Cover may be a separate policy or an additional benefit attached to another policy.

Compare definitions of total and partial disability, whether disability is permanent or temporary, how inability to continue your occupation is defined and any waiting period before benefits begin. Accident insurance may cover some accident-related disabilities; disability coverage varies by plan. Compare both policies' definitions and avoid counting the same benefit twice.

## 6. Vehicle insurance

In India, at least third-party liability insurance is required to use a vehicle on public roads. It covers specified legal liabilities for injury, death or property damage to others; it generally does not cover damage to your own vehicle.

- **Third-party insurance:** Liability cover required by law.
- **Own-damage insurance:** May cover damage to your vehicle from an accident, theft or other listed risks, subject to the policy.
- **Package or comprehensive policy:** May combine third-party and own-damage cover; verify the included cover in the policy schedule.

Compare deductibles, exclusions, vehicle valuation, repair networks, add-ons and how a claim affects the no-claim bonus. Keep valid registration and insurance documents.

## 7. Home insurance

Home insurance can cover the building, its contents or both. The extent of cover for fire, natural disasters, theft and other risks depends on the plan, sum insured and exclusions. Homeowners can consider building cover; tenants can explore cover for their own belongings.

Understand the sums insured for the building and contents separately. **Bharat Griha Raksha** is one of the standard residential property insurance products referenced by IRDAI; check its specific terms and available plans in the official documents. Ordinary wear and tear, lack of maintenance or certain risks may be excluded; the policy terms govern.

## What to compare before buying

- Which events trigger a benefit, and how are they defined?
- What are the sum insured, sub-limits, co-payments, deductibles and exclusions?
- What are the waiting periods, renewal rules and terms for premium changes?
- What are the claim deadlines, required documents and hospital or repair networks?
- Have you accurately disclosed your health, income, property or vehicle details in the application?

Do not assume similarly named plans provide identical cover. Read the policy documents and Customer Information Sheet, and ask the insurer to clarify unfamiliar definitions in writing. A qualified insurance or financial adviser can help you choose for your family and finances.

> This article is for education and awareness, not personal insurance or financial advice. Benefits, exclusions and claim conditions vary by product.
