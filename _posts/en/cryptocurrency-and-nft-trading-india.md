Cryptocurrencies and NFTs are digital assets recorded using technologies such as blockchain. Their prices can change rapidly; they are neither bank deposits nor government-guaranteed investments. Before buying such assets in India, understand the law, taxation, the platform's reliability and your personal financial risk.

## What is cryptocurrency trading?

Buying, selling or holding crypto tokens such as Bitcoin and Ether on an exchange is commonly called cryptocurrency trading. Some people buy tokens on the spot market and hold them in a wallet; some platforms also offer leveraged derivatives such as futures or perpetual contracts. Buying on the spot market and trading derivatives are different: derivatives can lead to losses greater than the margin initially deposited.

Crypto prices can change because of market demand, technological developments, security incidents, legal or tax announcements, liquidity and global market conditions. When prices fall, you could lose most or all of your investment.

## Choosing an exchange in India

Some crypto trading platforms widely known to users in India include [CoinDCX](https://coindcx.com/), [CoinSwitch](https://coinswitch.co/), [ZebPay](https://zebpay.com/), [Mudrex](https://mudrex.com/) and [Binance](https://www.binance.com/). **These names are neither a ranking nor a recommendation; platform availability and terms may change.** Before using any platform:

- Check its official announcements to confirm that it currently offers the service to customers in India.
- Check whether it is registered with FIU-IND as a Virtual Digital Asset Service Provider (VDA SP). FIU-IND registration relates to reporting and anti-money-laundering obligations under the PMLA; it is not government approval of an investment, investor compensation or a guarantee that funds are safe.
- Compare deposit and withdrawal fees, trading fees, spreads, withdrawal times, complaint-resolution procedures and account-security features.
- When transferring assets to a personal wallet, check the network, address and private-key security. A crypto transaction sent to the wrong address may not be recoverable.

Check registrations and notices through FIU-IND's [notice on VDA service providers](https://fiuindia.gov.in/pdfs/downloads/VDASP04072023.pdf) and [official website](https://fiuindia.gov.in/). Registration does not mean an exchange is safe or profitable. Beware of fake apps, phishing links, promises of guaranteed returns and social-media "signal" groups.

## What is an NFT?

A Non-Fungible Token (NFT) is a token recorded on a blockchain with a unique identity. It may be associated with digital art, music, a collectible or another claim of ownership. However, buying an NFT does not automatically give you copyright, commercial-use rights or the original file of the artwork. Read the licence and the rights offered by the seller before buying.

NFTs are generally listed on NFT marketplaces; a crypto exchange and an NFT marketplace are not the same. Consider risks including the authenticity of the NFT, where its metadata is stored, blockchain/network fees, resale liquidity, marketplace closure and fake collections. Resale royalties and future buyers are not guaranteed.

## Tax and legal position

Crypto and NFT transactions may be subject to India's Virtual Digital Asset (VDA) and tax rules. Tax, TDS and reporting requirements can vary depending on the asset, the transaction and the law in force. This article does not provide specific tax rates or legal advice; consult official tax-department guidance and a qualified tax professional before transacting.

Do not assume that crypto or NFTs are legal tender in India or government-guaranteed assets. FIU-IND registration, taxation or an exchange listing does not constitute government approval of an investment. Check current FEMA/RBI rules and permitted routes applicable to Indian residents; understand the additional legal risks before using unregulated offshore platforms.

## Basic security checklist

- Consider putting only an amount whose loss would not affect everyday expenses, loan instalments or emergency savings into risky digital assets.
- Never share an OTP, seed phrase, private key or wallet recovery phrase with anyone, including someone claiming to be support staff.
- Enable security features such as two-factor authentication (2FA), a unique password and a withdrawal allowlist where available.
- Keep transaction records, purchase prices, fees, wallet transfers and sale details for tax reporting.
- Before trading with borrowed money or leverage, fully understand how losses can be magnified.
