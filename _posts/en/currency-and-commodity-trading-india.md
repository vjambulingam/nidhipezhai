Currency and commodity trading are market activities used to protect against price changes (hedging) or to take positions based on expectations of market prices. In India, these activities can take place through derivative contracts listed on recognised stock exchanges. This is not the same as exchanging currency at a bank or buying gold or raw materials directly.

## 1. Currency trading

Currency trading on Indian exchanges generally involves buying or selling contracts such as currency futures and options. Exchange rules specify the underlying currency pair, contract size, tick size, expiry, margin and settlement method. People with foreign-currency expenses or income from business or investments may hedge to manage exchange-rate risk; taking speculative positions is also highly risky.

The list of available and permitted INR-related currency pairs and cross-currency contracts can change. Currency derivatives trading in India is subject to RBI/FEMA rules, SEBI regulation, exchange rules and applicable exposure and eligible-contract conditions. Avoid unauthorised offshore forex platforms and apps promising guaranteed profits, and verify current rules through official sources.

### Markets offering currency derivatives in India

- [National Stock Exchange of India (NSE)](https://www.nseindia.com/products-services/about-currency-derivatives) — currency derivatives segment.
- [BSE](https://www.bseindia.com/) — see the Currency Derivatives market section and current contracts.
- [Metropolitan Stock Exchange of India (MSEI)](https://www.msei.in/products/currency-derivatives/default) — currency futures and options market information.

Do not assume that the same currency pair or contract is available on every exchange. Before trading, check the contracts listed that day, lot size, expiry, margin and settlement details.

## 2. Commodity trading

Commodity derivatives prices are linked to underlying commodities such as gold, silver, crude oil, natural gas, base metals or agricultural products. They are commonly traded as futures or options contracts. Global markets, exchange rates, weather, harvests, storage, transport, government policy, and supply and demand can change prices quickly.

Depending on the contract, settlement may be in cash or through physical delivery. Do not hold a contract without understanding its delivery period, tender period, warehouse, quality standards, delivery centre and expiry rules. You could incur unwanted delivery or settlement obligations.

### Markets offering commodity derivatives in India

- [Multi Commodity Exchange of India (MCX)](https://www.mcxindia.com/market-data/market-watch) — bullion, energy, metals and other listed commodity contracts.
- [National Commodity & Derivatives Exchange (NCDEX)](https://ncdex.com/) — agricultural and related commodity derivatives contracts.
- [NSE Commodity Derivatives](https://www.nseindia.com/products-services/about-commodity-derivatives) — NSE's commodity derivatives segment.
- [BSE](https://www.bseindia.com/) — check the Commodity Derivatives section for the current list of contracts.

Not every commodity is available on every exchange. Before deciding, check each contract's specifications, trading hours, lot size, price limits, margin, delivery or settlement method, and exchange notices.

## 3. What to understand before trading

- **Futures:** A contract carries an obligation to buy or sell according to its terms at expiry. Do not assume the initial margin is the maximum possible loss; market movements may require additional margin.
- **Options:** The buyer pays a premium for a right; the seller has a contractual obligation. Clearly understand the premium, expiry and option-writing risk.
- **Margin and leverage:** A small initial amount can support a large notional position. This can rapidly magnify losses as well as profits.
- **Mark-to-market:** Price movements can lead to daily financial adjustments or requests for additional margin.
- **Costs:** Account for brokerage, exchange and clearing fees, taxes and other expenses.
- **Hedging:** Derivatives may be used to reduce an actual currency or commodity exposure, but a hedge is neither complete protection nor a guaranteed profit.

Trade in India only through a SEBI-registered broker. Read the contract documents, risk disclosures and exchange circulars; do not take a trade based solely on a price tip or social-media advice.

> This article is for education and awareness only; it is not personal investment or trading advice. Futures and options can result in the loss of part or all of an investment, and losses can exceed the margin. Income and profits are not guaranteed.
