An **Infrastructure Investment Trust (InvIT)** gives investors a way to participate in infrastructure assets that may generate distributions and offer potential for capital appreciation. Unlike a bank fixed deposit (FD), an InvIT does not promise a fixed return, and its unit price and distributions can change.

## 1. What is an Infrastructure Investment Trust?

An **InvIT** is an investment structure that pools money from investors and invests in infrastructure assets. It shares some structural similarities with a mutual fund or a real estate investment trust (REIT), though these products invest in different assets and have different risks.

- **How it works:** The trust invests in infrastructure assets that can generate income over time.
- **Assets:** These may include toll roads, power transmission lines, telecom towers, gas pipelines and renewable energy projects.
- **SEBI rules:** Subject to applicable regulations, an InvIT must distribute at least **90% of its net distributable cash flows (NDCF)** to unitholders. Distributions can include dividends, interest or repayment of capital, depending on the trust's cash flows and structure. Distribution frequency and other requirements depend on the applicable rules and the trust's terms; a distribution is not a guaranteed return.

---

## 2. How can you invest in an InvIT?

Retail investors can access eligible publicly offered or exchange-traded InvITs:

1. **Demat and trading account:** Use a SEBI-registered broker, such as Zerodha, Groww, Upstox, Angel One or HDFC Sky.
2. **Exchange trading:** Units of eligible listed InvITs can be bought and sold on an exchange such as NSE or BSE, much like shares, subject to availability and liquidity.
3. **Minimum investment:** Some publicly traded InvIT units may be purchased in quantities as small as one unit. Prices vary; ₹100–₹150 per unit is an example, not a current quote or a universal minimum. Brokerage and other charges also apply.
4. **Public offer:** Investors can also apply for an eligible new InvIT public offer, subject to its offer terms, minimum application size and allotment.

Not every InvIT or issue is available to retail investors on the same terms. Check the offer documents and exchange information before investing.

## 3. Comparison of some InvITs in India

The following examples illustrate different infrastructure exposures. This is not a complete list, and the figures are illustrative estimates from the original article, **not current or guaranteed yields**. Check current disclosures, eligibility and trading availability before making a decision.

| InvIT | Sector | Main assets | Revenue model | Illustrative distribution yield |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **PowerGrid InvIT (PGInvIT)** | Power transmission | Inter-state transmission lines | Long-term transmission arrangements; subject to contract and counterparty risks | ~10%–11.5% |
| **India Grid Trust (IndiGrid)** | Power transmission and solar | Transmission systems and solar plants | Transmission and power-related contracts; terms and risks vary by asset | ~10%–11% |

## 4. Which type of InvIT might suit an investor?

### a) IndiGrid and PowerGrid InvIT

Investors seeking regular cash flow, including retirees, may wish to examine transmission-oriented InvITs. Transmission revenue depends on contractual and regulatory arrangements rather than road traffic, but it is still exposed to operational, counterparty and other risks. Distributions and capital are not guaranteed.

### b) IRB InvIT Fund

An investor comfortable with road-sector risk may consider a highway InvIT. Toll revenue can be affected by economic activity and traffic. Toll revisions may support revenue over time, but neither distributions nor capital appreciation are assured.

### c) NHIT

An investor interested in NHAI-sponsored infrastructure may consider NHIT, subject to the availability and terms of the specific offering. Government-related sponsorship does not eliminate investment risk or guarantee returns.

These are examples, not recommendations. Assess each trust's assets, debt, terms, valuation and suitability for your circumstances.

## 5. Potential advantages of InvITs

1. **Potential distribution yield:** An InvIT may offer a higher distribution yield than an FD. The original article cited an illustrative range of 8%–11%, but actual distributions vary and can include capital repayment.
2. **Cash-flow distribution:** SEBI's applicable rules require distribution of at least 90% of NDCF, but the amount available to distribute can change.
3. **Potential inflation linkage:** Some toll or infrastructure tariffs may be revised over time. The terms vary, and inflation protection is not guaranteed.
4. **Portfolio diversification:** InvITs can provide access to infrastructure assets without the large sums needed to own a project directly. Diversification does not eliminate losses.
5. **Disclosure and regulation:** InvITs are subject to SEBI regulations and applicable disclosure and audit requirements.

---

## 6. Disadvantages and risks

1. **Interest-rate risk:** Rising interest rates can put downward pressure on InvIT unit prices, although prices depend on other factors too.
2. **Traffic and operational risk:** A slowdown or alternative routes can reduce toll collections. Infrastructure assets also face operating and maintenance risks.
3. **Tax complexity:** Distributions may consist of interest, dividends or capital repayment, with different tax treatment. Rules depend on the trust's structure, the investor's circumstances and current law.
4. **Uncertain capital appreciation:** Because a large share of distributable cash flow is paid out, growth may depend on acquisitions, financing and other factors. Unit prices can also fall.
5. **Liquidity risk:** Some units may trade infrequently, making it harder to buy or sell a large holding at the desired price.

---

## Conclusion

InvITs can be one way to add infrastructure exposure and potential cash flow to a portfolio, but they are not substitutes for guaranteed fixed-interest investments. The original article suggested an allocation of 5%–15%; this is not a universal recommendation. Consider your goals, risk tolerance and diversification before choosing an allocation.

Before investing, review the trust's debt, the remaining life of its concessions, asset performance, distribution composition and tax implications, along with its latest disclosures.
