Earning money is important, as is making a plan for what you save. Inflation can reduce the purchasing power of money over time, including money kept in a savings account if its interest does not keep pace with rising prices.

This guide explains how to start investing in a planned way. It is general educational information, not a personal recommendation to buy a particular investment.

## 1. Build your financial foundation first

Before investing, review your finances using the [personal finance fortification checklist](../../checklists/personal-finance-fortification-checklist.html).

1. **Pay down high-interest debt:** Prioritise credit-card balances or other expensive debt, considering interest, fees, prepayment terms and your cash flow. The [credit-card payoff calculator](../../calculators/credit-card-payoff-calculator.html) can help you explore repayment.
2. **Build an emergency fund:** Set aside at least three to six months of essential expenses for events such as job loss or medical emergencies. Unstable income or greater family responsibilities may call for more. Use the [emergency fund planner](../../calculators/emergency-fund-planner.html) to estimate your needs.
3. **Consider insurance:** People with family responsibilities should assess appropriate term life and health cover. Compare the sum insured, exclusions, waiting periods, premiums and policy conditions against your needs. The [life insurance needs calculator](../../calculators/life-insurance-sum-assured-calculator.html) and [health insurance calculator](../../calculators/medical-insurance-sum-assured-calculator.html) can help with estimates.

## 2. Define your financial goals

Write down how much you need and when for each goal, such as an emergency fund, education, a home down payment or retirement.

* **Short term, up to five years:** Because the money will be needed sooner, consider whether highly volatile investments are appropriate.
* **Medium term, more than five and up to ten years:** Choose your level of risk based on how close the goal is and your capacity for loss.
* **Long term, more than ten years:** A long horizon does not eliminate market risk. Choose an asset allocation suited to the goal, income stability and capacity for loss.

These periods are general guides, not rules that assign one investment to every goal. Use the [goal-based investing calculator](../../calculators/goal-based-investing-calculator.html) to estimate a target amount and time horizon.

## 3. Understand investment options

Consider each product's purpose, risk, costs, tax and liquidity. Do not assume a product is low-risk or high-return based on its name.

* **Bank fixed deposit (FD):** The interest rate and term are specified in advance. Early withdrawal may reduce interest or incur charges. Eligible bank deposits have DICGC insurance subject to its limits and conditions; do not assume every deposit is fully insured. Explore the [FD calculator](../../calculators/fd_returns_calculator.html).
* **Public Provident Fund (PPF):** A long-term government small-savings scheme with an initial 15-year term. Money needed for a short-term goal may not be suitable for it. Check eligibility, withdrawal, extension and tax rules. Use the [PPF calculator](../../calculators/ppf_returns_calculator.html).
* **Mutual funds:** These pool investors' money and invest in shares, debt or a mix of assets according to the scheme. Some are actively managed; index funds follow an index. Review the Riskometer, objective, expense ratio, exit load, tax and risks. Principal and returns are not guaranteed.
* **Systematic investment plan (SIP):** A method of investing in a mutual fund at regular intervals, not a separate investment product or a guarantee of returns. Minimum amounts vary by scheme and platform. The [SIP calculator](../../calculators/mf_sip_calculator.html) can illustrate different amounts and periods, but its projections are not guarantees.
* **Index funds:** These follow an index such as the Nifty 50 or Sensex and may have lower costs than some actively managed funds. Review market risk, concentration, expenses and tracking error. “Index” does not mean capital protection.
* **Gold ETFs and Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs):** Gold prices fluctuate, and gold itself does not pay interest. Gold ETFs generally require a demat account for exchange trading; check costs and tracking. SGBs have their own interest and redemption terms, and their returns and market prices are not guaranteed. Check availability, secondary-market liquidity, redemption and tax rules before buying. Neither product is direct jewellery ownership.
* **Direct shares:** A company's share price can rise or fall, and an investment in one company can be lost entirely. If you lack the time or experience to research companies, consider whether direct shares are appropriate.

For more detail, read the [guide to equity and market-linked products](../equity-investments-in-india/) and [how to build an investment portfolio](../portfolio-creation-approach/).

## 4. Practical steps to begin

Check that your name and address are consistent across your PAN and Aadhaar records, and correct discrepancies where needed. Required documents and verification procedures vary by investment and provider.

1. **Budget and choose an amount:** Start with an amount you can invest regularly without compromising essential expenses or emergencies. There is no universal minimum.
2. **Choose the account and complete KYC:** PAN and KYC details are required for many investments. Mutual funds generally do not require demat or trading accounts and can be purchased through an AMC. Exchange-traded shares and ETFs generally require both accounts.
3. **Verify authorisation and costs:** Use a SEBI-registered intermediary or the relevant AMC's official platform. Compare brokerage, platform fees, expense ratios and other charges. Never share an OTP, PIN or password.
4. **Start with what you understand:** Read product documents, the Riskometer where applicable, costs, exit rules and tax implications. SIPs and other market-linked investments can lose value.
5. **Review periodically:** Rather than reacting to daily price changes, review your allocation at planned intervals and when goals or income change. Reassess the risk of money needed as a goal approaches.

## 5. Common mistakes to avoid

* **Trusting get-rich-quick schemes:** Be wary of promises to double money in a short time.
* **Following tips blindly:** Do not buy shares solely on recommendations from friends or social media.
* **Concentrating in one asset or share:** Diversification across appropriate assets can help manage risk but does not eliminate losses.
* **Assuming returns are guaranteed:** Past returns and compounding projections do not guarantee future results.
* **Lacking patience:** Markets fluctuate in the short term. Long-term investing offers opportunities for growth and compounding, but profits are not guaranteed.
* **Acting on pressure or rumours:** Do not transfer money based on promises of quick or guaranteed profit or advice from someone whose registration has not been verified. If needed, read our [guide to SEBI-registered investment advisers](../sebi-registered-investment-advisors/).

## Remember

Understand your goal, time horizon, risks, costs and withdrawal rules before investing. There is no need to rush into a product you do not understand. The [inflation calculator](../../calculators/inflation-calculator.html) can help illustrate inflation's long-term effect.
