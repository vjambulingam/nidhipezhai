When you start investing in mutual funds, you may notice two versions of the same scheme, for example:

1. **HDFC Top 100 Fund - Regular Plan - Growth**
2. **HDFC Top 100 Fund - Direct Plan - Growth**

Both plans belong to the same scheme, share its underlying portfolio and are managed by the same fund manager. Their expenses differ, however, and even a modest difference in annual returns can compound into a substantial amount over 20 or 25 years. The actual difference depends on the amount invested, expenses, returns and holding period.

This article explains how the plans differ, illustrates the potential impact of a 1% annual return gap, and describes how to identify your plan and consider a switch.

## 1. What is a regular plan?

A regular plan is typically purchased through a **mutual fund distributor**, such as a bank, agent or broker.

* The fund company pays distributor commission, including trail commission, for regular-plan investments. Rates vary by scheme and distributor; the original article gives an illustrative range of around 0.5%–1.5% annually.
* The plan's expenses are reflected in its net asset value (NAV), rather than normally appearing as a separate commission debit from your account. Trail commission can continue while the investment remains in the plan, subject to applicable arrangements.
* As a result, a regular plan generally has a higher **total expense ratio (TER)** than the direct plan of the same scheme.

---

## 2. What is a direct plan?

Direct plans were introduced in India in 2013 under SEBI's framework. They allow investors to invest without a mutual fund distributor, for example through the asset management company (AMC) or a platform offering direct plans.

* Direct plans do not include distributor commissions in their expenses.
* Their TER is generally lower than that of the corresponding regular plan. Other fund expenses still apply, and a platform or adviser may charge separately.
* Lower expenses leave more of the scheme's return for the investor, and the difference can compound over time. Returns are not guaranteed and the expense gap can change.

---

## 3. The long-term effect of a 1% return difference

A 1% annual difference may seem small, but its effect can compound over a long investment period.

### Example: ₹10,000 monthly SIP for 25 years

The table assumes a fixed monthly investment made at the **beginning** of each month, with constant annual returns divided by 12 for monthly compounding. The returns shown are hypothetical **net returns**, not forecasts.

| Comparison | Regular plan | Direct plan | Difference |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Monthly SIP** | ₹10,000 | ₹10,000 | — |
| **Investment period** | 25 years | 25 years | — |
| **Total invested** | ₹30,00,000 | ₹30,00,000 | — |
| **Assumed annual return** | 12% | 13% | 1 percentage point |
| **Estimated value after 25 years** | ₹1,89,76,351 | ₹2,27,14,350 | ₹37,37,999 |

This difference is the combined effect of the assumed return gap and compounding; it is **not the amount of commission paid to a distributor**. Actual returns fluctuate, expense differences vary and taxes, exit loads and any separately charged fees are not included. The original article's direct-plan estimate of ₹2,29,86,115 does not match these stated calculation assumptions; the table uses the recalculated amount.

## 4. Regular vs direct: a comparison

| Feature | Regular plan | Direct plan |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Investment route** | Bank, agent or mutual fund distributor | AMC or a platform offering direct plans |
| **Distributor commission** | Included in plan expenses; rates vary | No distributor commission in the plan |
| **TER** | Generally higher, for example 1.80% | Generally lower, for example 0.80% |
| **NAV** | A separate NAV is published | A separate NAV is published; its absolute value alone does not determine which plan is better |
| **Returns** | Returns reflect regular-plan expenses | Returns typically benefit from lower expenses; the actual gap varies |
| **Support and suitability** | A distributor may provide assistance with transactions and scheme selection | Suitable for investors comfortable making decisions themselves or obtaining separate advice |

TER figures are examples, not current rates. Compare the same scheme and option, review its current disclosures, and consider the value and cost of any support you need.

## 5. How can you identify your plan?

Open your mutual fund account statement, consolidated account statement (CAS) or investment app:

1. Check the full scheme name for **Direct Plan**, for example *Parag Parikh Flexi Cap Fund - Direct Plan - Growth*.
2. If it says **Regular Plan**, it is a regular plan. If the name is abbreviated or does not specify the plan, confirm with the AMC or check the detailed statement rather than assuming.
3. Investments arranged through a bank may be regular plans, but verify the actual plan for each holding.

---

## 6. How can you switch from a regular to a direct plan?

If you hold a regular plan, consider both future contributions and existing units before changing:

1. **Future SIPs:** You may stop the existing regular-plan SIP and start a new SIP in the direct plan of the same scheme. Check the registration and cancellation dates so you understand when each instruction takes effect. Stopping an SIP does not redeem existing units.
2. **Existing units:** A switch from regular to direct typically redeems units in the regular plan and purchases units in the direct plan. You may be able to request this through the AMC or a platform such as MF Central, subject to its supported features.
3. **Check the costs and tax consequences first:**
   * **Exit load:** The applicable load depends on the scheme and how long each unit has been held. Some equity schemes charge 1% for redemptions within a year, but check the scheme's current terms and your individual purchase dates.
   * **Capital gains tax:** A switch is generally treated as a redemption for tax purposes. Tax depends on the fund category, acquisition dates, holding periods, gains and current law. For eligible listed equity-oriented fund gains, the original article cites a ₹1.25 lakh annual long-term capital gains threshold; it applies across relevant gains rather than separately to each switch. Check current rules and your circumstances before planning a phased switch or consult a tax professional.

Do not switch solely because a direct plan has a lower expense ratio: weigh exit loads, tax, platform or advice fees and the support you need.

---

## Conclusion

Small differences in expenses can have a meaningful effect over time. Review your holdings, compare the direct and regular plans of the same scheme, and make any change only after considering its costs and tax consequences.
